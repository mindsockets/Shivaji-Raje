His own family

Shivaji Maharaj married eight times by the usual count, as kings of his age often did, mostly to bind powerful families to swarajya. He had two sons and, by the usual count, six daughters. Below are his queens, his children, and his grandsons, all born after his death, who carried the crown into the next century.

How sure is this?

Parents, brothers, the two sons, and the grandsons are well recorded. The four best-recorded queens are Saibai, Soyarabai, Putalabai and Sakvarbai. For the others, and for some queens' families (Sarkar, for one, calls Putalabai a Mohite), the sources vary, and the marriage years are approximate. The daughters' names, their mothers and their husbands come from family histories that vary. Dates of this period are often in the Old Style calendar, ten days behind ours.

Two things here are uncertain. Maloji's wife is usually named Umabai, but the story about her and the naming of Shahaji and Sharifji comes from the Shiva Digvijay, a text historians regard as late. The birth years of Ekoji and the elder Sambhaji also vary between sources.

Families woven together

Marriages tied the great Maratha families to the Bhosales. The same surnames keep coming back, in the family and on the battlefield.

Mohite मोहितेTukabai (Shahaji's wife); Soyarabai (Shivaji's queen); her brother Hambirrao, the senapati; his daughter Tarabai, who married Rajaram.
Jadhav जाधवJijabai, daughter of Lakhuji Jadhav of Sindkhed; Kashibai, one of the queens.
Nimbalkar निंबाळकरSaibai of Phaltan, the first queen; her nephew Mahadji (in most accounts) married Shivaji's daughter Sakhubai.
Shirke शिर्केSagunabai, a queen; Yesubai, Sambhaji's wife; Ganoji Shirke, who married Shivaji's daughter Rajkunwarbai.
