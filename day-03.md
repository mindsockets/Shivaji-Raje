# Day 3 — Jijabai's Lap

*Act 1 — The Dream is Born · `[C]` context / bridge · his first teacher*

**Tonight:** we meet the person who planted the dream of *Swarajya* in a small boy's heart —
his mother, **Jijabai**, his first and greatest teacher.

---

## The telling (English)

Two nights ago we found the dream — **Swarajya**. Last night the little dreamer was born and
named **Shivaji**. But a dream needs someone to plant it in a child's heart. Tonight, meet the
person who did: his mother, **Jijabai**.

When Shivaji was still small, Jijabai brought him down from the fort to a town called
**Pune**, to a home called the **Lal Mahal** — the "Red Palace." But Pune was not a happy
place. Wars had rolled through it; fields lay broken, houses burned, and the people were
afraid.

So Jijabai did something wonderful. To show everyone that life could begin again, she had the
tired old land ploughed with a shining **golden ploughshare**, and planted the first seeds with
her own hands. Slowly, Pune woke up. Gardens grew. Markets opened. Children laughed in the
lanes again. And little Shivaji watched his mother heal a whole town — and learned, without a
single lecture, what a good ruler is *for*.

And oh, the stories she told him! Every evening, curled in her lap, he drank in the old, old
tales — of **Ram**, who kept his promise no matter the cost; of the brave **Pandavas**; of
kings who protected the weak and never, ever bowed to bullies. Shivaji didn't just want to
*hear* about heroes. He wanted to *be* one.

She gave him teachers, too. A wise man named **Dadoji Konddev** helped run the lands and showed
him how a country is governed — how to be fair, how to keep honest accounts, how to actually
*listen* to farmers. And Shivaji learned to ride a horse, to swing a sword, and to read the
mountains like a map. He grew strong, and quick, and kind.

But the deepest lessons were the ones from his mother's lap: *Be just. Be brave. Protect your
people. This land is worth fighting for.* Little by little, those words were turning a dream
into a promise.

By now Jijabai was far more than a mother. She was his first teacher — his first **guru**. The
people loved her so dearly that they gave her a fond, warm name: **Jijau**.

So tonight there is no battle — only a boy, his mother, and a lapful of stories quietly shaping
the king he would become.

*Goodnight — tonight's badge: **Jijau, "the first teacher."***

*Tomorrow night: the wild, brave friends who would become his brothers.*

## गोष्ट (मराठी)

दोन रात्रींपूर्वी आपल्याला स्वप्न सापडलं — **स्वराज्य.** काल तो चिमुकला स्वप्नाळू जन्माला आला आणि
त्याचं नाव ठेवलं **शिवाजी.** पण स्वप्न रुजवायला कुणीतरी लागतं ना — लहानग्याच्या मनात. आज भेटूया
त्याच व्यक्तीला: त्याची आई, **जिजाबाई.**

शिवाजी अगदी लहान असताना, जिजाबाईंनी त्याला किल्ल्यावरून खाली **पुणे** नावाच्या गावी आणलं, **लाल
महाल** नावाच्या घरी. पण तेव्हा पुणे सुखी नव्हतं. तिथून युद्धं गेली होती; शेतं उद्ध्वस्त, घरं जळलेली,
आणि माणसं भ्यायलेली.

म्हणून जिजाबाईंनी एक फार सुंदर गोष्ट केली. आयुष्य पुन्हा सुरू होऊ शकतं हे साऱ्यांना दाखवायला,
त्यांनी त्या थकल्या भूमीवर चकाकणाऱ्या **सोन्याच्या नांगराने** नांगर धरला आणि स्वतःच्या हातांनी पहिलं
बी पेरलं. हळूहळू पुणे जागं झालं. बागा फुलल्या. बाजार भरले. गल्ल्यांमध्ये पुन्हा मुलं हसू लागली. आणि
छोटा शिवाजी आपल्या आईला अख्खं गाव पुन्हा उभं करताना बघत होता — आणि एकही उपदेश न ऐकता शिकत
होता, की चांगला राजा *कशासाठी* असतो.

आणि त्या किती गोष्टी सांगायच्या! रोज संध्याकाळी, त्यांच्या कुशीत, तो त्या जुन्या-जुन्या कथा ऐकायचा —
वचन पाळणाऱ्या **रामाच्या**, शूर **पांडवांच्या**, दुर्बलांचं रक्षण करणाऱ्या आणि गुंडांपुढे कधीही न
झुकणाऱ्या राजांच्या. शिवाजीला वीरांबद्दल नुसतं *ऐकायचं* नव्हतं — त्याला स्वतः वीर *व्हायचं* होतं.

त्यांनी त्याला गुरुही दिले. **दादोजी कोंडदेव** नावाच्या शहाण्या माणसाने कारभार सांभाळला आणि त्याला
शिकवलं — राज्य कसं चालवायचं, न्यायी कसं राहायचं, प्रामाणिक हिशोब कसे ठेवायचे, आणि शेतकऱ्यांचं
खरंखुरं कसं *ऐकायचं.* आणि शिवाजी घोडा दौडवायला, तलवार चालवायला, आणि डोंगर नकाशासारखे वाचायला
शिकला. तो बलवान, चपळ आणि दयाळू होत गेला.

पण सर्वांत खोल शिकवण आईच्या कुशीतलीच होती: *न्यायी हो. धाडसी हो. आपल्या माणसांचं रक्षण कर. ही
भूमी लढण्यासारखी आहे.* हळूहळू ते शब्द स्वप्नाचं रूपांतर एका वचनात करत होते.

आतापर्यंत जिजाबाई नुसत्या आई राहिल्या नव्हत्या. त्या त्याच्या पहिल्या गुरु होत्या. लोकांचं त्यांच्यावर
इतकं प्रेम, की त्यांनी त्यांना एक लाडकं नाव दिलं — **जिजाऊ.**

तर आज रात्री युद्ध नाही — फक्त एक मुलगा, त्याची आई, आणि कुशीभर गोष्टी, जो पुढे राजा होणार, त्याला
हळूच घडवणाऱ्या.

*शुभ रात्री — आजचं जपायचं नाणं: **जिजाऊ, "पहिली गुरु."***

*उद्या रात्री: पुढे भाऊ होणारे ते रांगडे, शूर मित्र.*

## Fact vs legend

- `[H]` **History:** **Jijabai** was the decisive influence of Shivaji's childhood — his first
  teacher, raising him at the **Lal Mahal** in **Pune**, which was administered as the family
  jagir. **Dadoji Konddev** managed the estate and contributed to his early education.
- `[L]` **Legend / symbol:** the **golden ploughshare** used to re-plough and resettle devastated
  Pune is the beloved ballad layer — Pune's revival under the family is historical; the gold plough
  is the storyteller's image.
- `[C]` **Framing:** the epics as his "schoolbooks" and the exact evening scenes are a warm
  reconstruction — but the *ideals* of just, protective kingship genuinely shaped him.
- **Debated:** the precise extent of Dadoji Konddev's role (and whether he was a formal "guru") is
  discussed by historians.
