The eight ministers — Ashtapradhan

The council of eight formed at the coronation (Day 22 · 26) — swarajya's government.

OfficeMinisterPortfolio
PeshwaMoropant Trimbak PinglePrime minister — head of the council
AmatyaRamchandra NilkanthFinance & revenue
SachivAnnaji DattoSecretary — correspondence & records
MantriDattaji TrimbakInterior & the royal chronicle
SenapatiHambirrao MohiteCommander-in-chief of the army
SumantRamchandra TrimbakForeign affairs
NyayadhishNiraji RavjiChief justice
PanditraoRaghunath PanditReligious & charitable affairs

A sourcing note: the eight offices are certain, but several ministers' personal names are recorded differently across sources (period bakhars vs later compilations) — e.g. the Panditrao is given as Raghunath Pandit or Moreshwar Pandit, the Sumant as Ramchandra Trimbak or Sonopant Dabir, and the Amatya (Ramchandra Nilkanth) is the same man later famed as Ramchandra Pant Amatya.

Voices that tell the story

The chroniclers, travellers, historians and poets whose words the nights quote or weigh — so you can see where each story comes from.

Krishnaji Anant Sabhasad सभासदDay 18 · 19
Courtier whose chronicle (bakhar), written in 1697, is a key early Maratha source.
Narayan Shenvi नारायण शेणवीDay 19
English factory interpreter; his letter of 4 April 1674 describes Prataprao's charge.
François BernierDay 14
French traveller who recorded who was spared in the sack of Surat.
Khafi KhanDay 10
Mughal-side Persian chronicler whose account of Pratapgad differs from the Maratha one.
James Grant DuffDay 10 · 20
British author of A History of the Mahrattas (1826).
Jadunath SarkarDay 10
Historian whose Shivaji and His Times weighed the evidence on Pratapgad.
Henry OxendenDay 22
English envoy whose 1674 diary is an eyewitness account of the coronation.
Kusumagraj कुसुमाग्रजDay 19
20th-century Marathi poet of "वेडात मराठे वीर दौडले सात".
